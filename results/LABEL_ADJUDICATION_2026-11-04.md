## What the adjudicated key is

The v1.4 key gives each kit case the label of its simulated facts, whatever level it is shown at. The kit asks the labeller to decide "using only the evidence shown" under rulebook v1.4, which (section 2) lets no non-conformity finding, or its denial, rest only on one party's own record, refers an authorisation dispute to the payer's PSP, and (section 4a) treats a missing delivery date as an unresolved withdrawal right. Where the facts that decide a case are shown only at a higher level, the key assumes facts the labeller cannot see. The adjudicated key therefore keeps the v1.4 label where the evidence shown at that level establishes it, and gives `undetermined` where it does not. A party's statement against its own interest (a buyer admitting a change of mind, or quoting an instruction that never named what it now wants) establishes what it concedes; a statement in its own favour does not. A ground-truth error, where the v1.4 label misapplies the rulebook to the case's own facts, is corrected for the case at every level.

Amendment 11 pre-registered evidence ambiguity as "reported", with the ground truth unchanged. The adjudicated key is therefore a second, exploratory key beside the original: it measures whether a labeller decided what the shown evidence supports, while the original measures agreement with the simulated facts. The Phase 2 evidence-level design (H2, H5) takes the first sense on purpose: it scores every level against the simulated facts to measure how much of them each level of evidence recovers.

## Inputs to the standard (policy disagreements)

1. **Injected merchant-hosted content.** On the two kit cases where the log shows the agent obeying an instruction on the merchant's page (X40DAA, XBEBFB), all four labels that differ from the key put the loss on the agent provider (the founder and run 2 on X40DAA; both runs on XBEBFB): 3 classed as policy disagreements, run 1's as a probable slip. v1.4 puts it on the merchant (AG4 before AG1-P), on the ground that a participating merchant answers for what it hosts. The rule is the author's; the disagreement is substantive and should go to independent experts and to the standard's apportionment rules.
2. **Burden of proof when a merchant answers with its own record only.** The founder and run 2 decide non-conformity and non-delivery claims for the buyer when the merchant offers only its packing record (card-scheme practice for goods not received); v1.4 sends them to review. This reading accounts for 10 of the 30 differences from the adjudicated key (3 of the founder's 4, 7 of run 2's 21).
3. **Withdrawal dates.** v1.4 treats a missing delivery date as unresolved even when every date consistent with the record gives the same answer (XDFF82: dispute 11 days after the order). A bound (delivery on or after the order, before the dispute) would settle such cases without the date.
4. **Referential terms in signed text.** 'My usual printer ink' (D37) binds under v1.4 only if a reference to an unstated product counts as a term; the corrected key says it does. The standard's A4.1 Step 3 examples are all explicit attributes; a sentence on references ('my usual', 'the same as last time') would remove the doubt. The founder ruled on 2026-11-04 that the reference binds (addendum); the standard's wording is not changed by that ruling.

### Ruling 2: a tiered burden of proof for merchant-performance claims (rulebook v1.5, ABS-V1)

**The rule as implemented.** Rulebook v1.5 (`rulebook/AGENTIC_DISPUTE_RULEBOOK_v1.5.md`, section 2a, and `.json`) is v1.4 plus one rule, stated normatively in ABS v0.3 A5.7 and applied in A4.1 Step 1; the engine applies it with `decide(..., rulebook="1.5")` (tests: `tests/test_burden_tier.py`). A claim of non-delivery or non-conformity, and nothing else, that no record with independent authority for the contested fact decides (the merchant answered only with its own packing record, photos, listing or statements, and no carrier scan or weight, registry, inspection or tied certified capture supports either side) is decided by the order value. At or below the published threshold (default EUR 100 per order, per currency, inclusive), with the buyer under the cap (default 3 ABS-V1 refunds per buyer identity in 12 months, counted by the payer's PSP), the merchant refunds (AG3). Above the threshold or the cap, or with the order value or the count missing, the case goes to human review or an independent inspection, as under v1.4. It never overrides Step 0 and never applies to an agent-instruction, capture or Step 2 dispute, or to a claim that also alleges one; a record with independent authority for either side decides as before. The residual risk is bounded: fabricated claims cost at most the threshold times the cap per buyer identity per year (EUR 300 with the defaults), more for a person with several identities or PSPs whose counts are not shared. The defaults are a design proposal; nothing about the rule has been evaluated.

**How the re-score was done.** Exploratory, post hoc, author-side; no model call. `acs/burden_rescore.py` applies ABS-V1 to each stored cell (one case at one evidence level): the scope is read from the claim text by the engine's own reader (on the 63 stored cases it selects exactly the 16 merchant-performance cases, D01-D13 and D45-D47); every record with independent authority in the package built for that level is cited for the merchant on the contested fact and read by the engine's gate v3 readers; the order value is the case's transaction amount (all EUR). No stored case carries a buyer history, so every buyer counts zero earlier ABS-V1 refunds and the cap never binds in these data. Where ABS-V1 applies the label becomes AG3 / merchant / refund; elsewhere the v1.4 label stands. Nothing above this addendum is changed.
