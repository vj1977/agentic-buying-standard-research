Reference: the Agentic Dispute Rulebook v1.4 (the current core; matches the Agentic Buying Standard v0.3)
  1. Authorisation. If the mandate was not created by the account holder with strong authentication by a payment provider, or the charge was not made under it, the case goes to the payer's bank and is not decided here. If the agent provider's systems charged without a valid mandate, the agent provider refunds; if the merchant reused the credential or charged after expiry, the merchant refunds.
  2. Conformity. If the delivery does not match the offer frozen at checkout (model, specification, colour, condition, quantity, accessories, photos, price, fees, delivery), including non-delivery and counterfeits, the merchant is liable and the remedy is a refund. A non-conformity finding should not rest only on one side's own evidence (its photos, packing record or statements) that no independent record supports.
  3. Manipulative offer. If the purchase was caused by instructions in merchant-hosted content (listing, hosted reviews, profile), by a statement of fact in the merchant's content shown to be false (a reference price never charged, false scarcity, invented urgency) that the agent's log shows influenced the choice, or by items or fees the merchant added or pre-selected, the merchant is liable and the remedy is a refund.
  4. Agent fault. The agent provider is liable and the remedy is a refund if the agent broke a term captured in the signed mandate or Allowance Policy: a machine-readable field (budget, category, merchants, units, expiry, a required approval, a captured item requirement, a "clarify if ambiguous" flag), or signed text the user confirmed (the instruction text shown at the confirmation step, or a clarification the user answered with authentication); or if it guessed where it was told to ask.
  5. Capture failure. The agent provider is liable and the remedy is a refund if the user stated a requirement about the purchase only in the conversation (or in a clarification answered without authentication) that the agent should have captured into the mandate it drafted, and the purchase misses it. A preference the user left open is not such a requirement. If the agent provider cannot produce the conversation, this is decided against it.
  6. Otherwise the buyer bears it: "authorised but wrong" (a wish never stated), a change of mind, user error, or a false claim. The remedy then follows the withdrawal right below: "withdrawal" if it is available, "deny" if it is not, "undetermined" if the evidence shown does not settle it.

Withdrawal right (consumer, EU distance purchase): 14 days from the day the consumer receives the goods. Not available to business buyers, for goods made to the consumer's specification, for sealed hygiene goods or sealed audio, video or software unsealed after delivery, or for date-specific services. Digital content (for example a gift-card code) loses the right once delivery has begun only if the record shows all three of: the consumer's prior express consent, the consumer's acknowledgement of losing the right, and the trader's confirmation. If any of these is not in the evidence shown, or the buyer's status, the jurisdiction or the dates are missing or inconsistent (for example a dispute dated before delivery), choose "undetermined" for the remedy, not "deny".

Use your own judgment. Where you would allocate liability differently from these rules, say so in the notes. Evidence may be incomplete, self-serving or manipulated.
